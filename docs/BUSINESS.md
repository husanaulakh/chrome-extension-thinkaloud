# ThinkAloud — business case

## Customer pain

Students often remember that a question felt difficult, but not what they were thinking at the time. Existing study tools record answers, grades, or transcripts. They add friction during an exam and often require accounts or cloud storage. The missing layer is a private replay of the learner's reasoning.

## Product wedge

ThinkAloud records one continuous local audio file. Question tags are timestamps attached to that file, not separate recordings. That keeps the mental model simple, makes review fast, and lets a learner convert an audio-only session into a tagged session later.

## Differentiation

Every feature is free: local-first storage, no account, no ads, no page scraping, no answer judgment, and exportable data. The product earns trust by doing less with the user's study material. A future business can be built around optional tutoring workflows or paid hosted sync without putting core capture behind a paywall.

## First release success signals

- A first session starts in under 20 seconds.
- A learner can find a marked question in two clicks.
- A crash or permission issue never silently loses a session.
- Users understand what stays on-device without reading a policy page.

## Deliberate non-goals

No correctness scoring, answer extraction, forced transcription, webpage scraping, or social feed. Those features would weaken the product's trust promise and distract from the capture → review loop.
